Information in Logic — explainers

Five scroll-driven, self-contained explainers dramatizing mechanisms from the paper. Scroll to scrub through each one; scrolling back up reverses it smoothly.

Prefer one continuous read? The combined explainer weaves all five into a single narrative, with connective text between them.

  • Reasoning Amplifies Information

    Feynman once asked which single sentence would let a future civilization rebuild the most science. Shannon's classical model explains how to send that sentence reliably, but stops the instant it's decoded — this piece draws the one stage added back in: a receiver who reasons over what's received.

  • What a Sentence Means

    A sentence's meaning is a kernel — the set of possible worlds consistent with it. A weaker sentence's kernel contains a stronger one's, entailment as literal, visible containment: why a stronger sentence's kernel looks smaller.

  • Less is More

    Alice knows exactly what she wants Bob to end up able to prove. A short, pre-agreed list of candidate kernels lets her send less than either obvious strategy — while Bob ends up able to prove more than was strictly required.

  • No Need to Know

    Alice doesn't know what Bob already knows. She hashes her kernel into a short index; Bob rules out same-bin "confounders" using his own background knowledge.

  • The Price of Incorrect Information

    Alice and Bob packed for a trip using forecasts checked at different times. Being merely outdated costs little to fix — but a confidently wrong forecast costs far more, and the gap only grows as Bob gets more sure of it.
